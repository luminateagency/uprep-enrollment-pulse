💬 Quick translator, since these terms come up a lot: CPM is what it costs to put the ad in front of 1,000 people. CPC is what it costs each time someone clicks it. Neither one tells you if the campaign is working. Cost per lead is the number that matters, since it's what U Prep actually pays for each real family that reaches out.
ℹ Meta drives 156 leads on $2,102 in spend (about $13.47 per lead). Google drives 63 form starts on $645, off two keywords, detailed in the Google Ads Performance section below. Google is now the cheaper channel per lead at $10.24, while Meta is spending about 3.3x more, so it's still producing more total volume.
⚠ The $10,000 budget is paced to spend fully by Oct 7 (Fall Count Day, the state funding deadline), not evenly. Through Aug 30 (this week's pacing checkpoint), the pacing plan (from the official spend schedule) calls for about $4,300 spent; actual spend sits at $2,747, roughly $1,553 behind schedule. Both campaigns launched about 10 days later than the planned July 29 start, confirmed by each platform's own daily spend history: Meta's first real dollar was spent Aug 8, Google's Aug 11. That gap explains most of the shortfall.
