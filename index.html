💬 Quick translator, since these terms come up a lot: CPM is what it costs to put the ad in front of 1,000 people. CPC is what it costs each time someone clicks it. Neither one tells you if the campaign is working. Cost per lead is the number that matters, since it's what U Prep actually pays for each real family that reaches out.
ℹ Meta drives 158 leads on $1,667 in spend (about $10.55 per lead). Google drives 35 form starts on $436, off two keywords, detailed in the Google Ads Performance section below. Meta is the cheaper channel per lead right now, but it's also spending nearly 4x more, so it's naturally producing more volume too.
⚠ The $10,000 budget is paced to spend fully by Oct 7 (Fall Count Day, the state funding deadline), not evenly. Through Aug 23, the pacing plan (from the official spend schedule) calls for about $3,250 spent; actual spend sits at $2,102, roughly $1,148 behind schedule. Both campaigns launched about 10 days later than the planned July 29 start, confirmed by each platform's own daily spend history: Meta's first real dollar was spent Aug 8, Google's Aug 11. That gap explains most of the shortfall.

What This Means, What's Next

Insights, updated with this pull
🎥 Instagram is real, not a side experiment. 19 of 158 leads (about 12%) came through Instagram. Meta's own optimization tool recommends shifting more budget into Instagram Feed and Instagram Stories specifically, since both are already running efficiently. Next step: approve a modest Instagram budget bump.
📈 Spend is behind the plan, and Fall Count Day is the deadline. Through Aug 23, the official pacing plan called for about $3,250 spent; actual spend is $2,102, about $1,148 behind. Catching up before Oct 7 means raising daily budgets soon rather than waiting.
🔍 Google's biggest limit right now is budget, not ranking. The ads only show for 10.9% of eligible searches, and budget is the reason for most of that missed share. A bigger daily budget on this campaign would likely bring in more of that traffic right away.
📱 Most Meta leads never touch the website, and that's expected. Google Analytics shows only 54 sessions came from Meta ad clicks, but Meta reports 158 form submissions. Facebook and Instagram let people fill out the form without ever leaving the app, so GA4 can't see most of it. This isn't a tracking gap, it's just how in-app lead forms work.
📅 Interest Form Submissions data is a few days old. That panel depends on a manual CSV export from Finalsite, and the last one is from Aug 21. Worth deciding together: pull a fresh export more often, or look into whether Finalsite can send data automatically.
